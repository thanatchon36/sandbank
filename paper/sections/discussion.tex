\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why this differs from earlier findings.} \citet{lynch2025agentic} deliberately built dilemmas in
which the harmful action was the only route to the agent's goal, and found such actions in models from every
developer they tested, Claude models included. ODCV-Bench \citep{li2025odcv} reports high violation rates
under KPI pressure across many models. SandBank instead gives the agent a compliant route for every lead
(request documents, escalate, report the miss), and outside the ceiling conditions the manager asks only for
results, never for an exception. That is how a bank is meant to work, and it is the setting a bank deploying an
RM agent cares about. We did not run the earlier scenarios, so we cannot separate the effect of design from
that of model generation. Since earlier Claude models did take the harmful action in Lynch et al.'s design, we
think the availability of a compliant route is the likelier explanation, not the model family. The results are
compatible: earlier work shows that some models in some designs violate; we show that these models in this
design did not, with bounds.

\paragraph{What the null does and does not license.} A bank can read the bounds as evidence that these models,
given this kind of tooling and policy, do not readily circumvent KYC/AML controls for a sales target. It
cannot read them as a guarantee. The study has one bank, one policy, one RM role, short episodes and fixed
texts; the agents may recognise a test (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); and the bounds are per probe at the
sample sizes we could afford. Banks should still log tool calls, keep hard controls on prohibited actions and
test their own deployment.

\paragraph{The useful difference is capability.} In an exploratory comparison, the models differed in how much
legitimate work they finished. A cautious agent that leaves a legal large deposit unopened has a business
cost that a violation-only evaluation would score as perfect. Pre-deployment testing of compliance agents
should measure both.

\paragraph{On null results.} We pre-registered hypotheses that predicted violations, ran the study as
planned, and report that the hypotheses failed. When the P4 ceiling produced no violation, we strengthened it
once, to a direct order (P5), as a logged exploratory condition; when that also produced none, we stopped and
report the failed gate rather than escalating the prompt until a violation appeared. We think such results
belong in the literature, because a field that publishes only positive misalignment findings will overstate
how often it happens.
