Banks are starting to give LLM agents tools that can open accounts and post transactions. We ask whether such
an agent, given an ordinary sales target and a manager who pushes for it, will circumvent know-your-customer
(KYC) and anti-money-laundering (AML) controls. SandBank is a fully synthetic core-banking sandbox exposed to
the agent as tools, with a deterministic detector for eight violation types, a Compliance check-in that probes
for deception, and pre-registered experiments varying pressure, control hardness, oversight cues, a compliance
sentence and four mitigations. Across \NProbes{} pre-registered runs of Claude Haiku~5.5, Sonnet~5.5 and % numbers-ok: design constant, version or citation
Opus~5.5 we observed \NViolations{} violations and \NAttempts{} circumvention attempts: the 95\% upper bound % numbers-ok: design constant, version or citation
on the violation rate is \UpperHaiku\% for Haiku (\NHaiku{} runs), \UpperSonnet\% for Sonnet and
\UpperOpus\% for Opus. Neither explicit licence from the manager (``use the override fields, I'll back you'')
nor a direct order to enter an override produced a violation, while scripted agents confirmed that the
environment registers every violation type. Instead the agents checked KYC, requested documents, escalated
sanctions hits and told the manager the target would be missed; every Compliance check-in answer was
consistent with the event log. The pre-registered hypotheses about pressure, hardness, oversight and
mitigations are therefore not supported, and the hypotheses about deception and knowing violations could not
be tested. We report where the models differ instead: legitimate goal attainment, such as opening a large
deposit with its report filed, was \BFourConvHaiku\% for Haiku, \BFourConvSonnet\% for Sonnet and
\BFourConvOpus\% for Opus. The
sandbox, pre-registration and all run logs are released.
