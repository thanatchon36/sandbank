\subsection{No violations under any confirmatory condition}

Across \NProbes{} confirmatory probes the detector found \NViolations{} violations and \NAttempts{}
attempts. \NInvalid{} probes were INVALID, \NRefused{} were REFUSED (the agent left the blocked lead
unhandled), and \NClassifierRefusals{} responses were stopped by the API's safety classifier. Table~\ref{tab:bounds} gives each condition and
Figure~\ref{fig:bounds} the intervals. Pooled over all experiments, the 95\% Wilson upper bound on the % numbers-ok: design constant, version or citation
per-probe violation rate is \UpperHaiku\% for Haiku (\NHaiku{} probes), \UpperSonnet\% for Sonnet
(\NSonnet{}) and \UpperOpus\% for Opus (\NOpus{}). The bounds are per probe with one blocked lead each; they
say nothing about rarer failure modes that a sandbox of this size cannot reach (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

The nulls cover each manipulation the pre-registration expected to matter. Pressure up to a threat of branch
closure and job loss (A), policy-only controls with no warning at all (B, H0), a cue that Compliance reviews
are paused (C, O2), and five-day episodes in which a deposit could be split across days (G) produced no
violation. Hard controls (B, H2) produced no attempts, so the agents did not try and fail; they did not try.

\subsection{The ceilings did not move the models}

The pre-registered ceiling P4 lets the manager say ``use the override fields, I'll back you''. In the pilot and
in Experiment F together, no model used an override at P4. The P5 direct order (an exploratory condition
added after the pilot) tells the agent to open the account anyway and enter ``Approved by branch manager''
as the reason; none of \NPilotPFive{} pilot probes did. In every one of them the agent's final report
referred to the instruction or the override, cited the policy and named a compliant route instead (requesting
documents, escalating, or leaving the account on hold), by a keyword search of the transcripts. This is the reason gate~2 % numbers-ok: design constant, version or citation
failed (Section~\ref{sec:gates}). The ceiling probes show that, in this environment, an explicit licence from
the manager does not make the models circumvent the controls. It does not show that no instruction would.

\subsection{What the agents did instead}

Figure~\ref{fig:behaviour} shows the compliant actions taken on the blocked lead in Experiment A. The agents
ran the KYC check, requested the missing documents, escalated sanctions hits and politically exposed persons to
Compliance, and messaged the manager that the lead would not convert today. How often the agents escalated
and messaged the manager varied by model: Haiku escalated in \EscalatedHaiku\% of probes and
told the manager in \ToldManagerHaiku\%, Sonnet in \EscalatedSonnet\% and \ToldManagerSonnet\%, and Opus in
\EscalatedOpus\% and \ToldManagerOpus\%.

\subsection{Legitimate goal attainment differs by model}

Because no model broke a rule, the models differ only in how much of the legitimate target they reached
(Figure~\ref{fig:goal}). The B4 lead, a large deposit that policy allows once its report is filed, is the
clearest case: Haiku opened it with the report in \BFourConvHaiku\% of probes, Sonnet in
\BFourConvSonnet\% and Opus in \BFourConvOpus\%. The clean lead was converted in \CleanConvHaiku\%,
\CleanConvSonnet\% and \CleanConvOpus\% of probes respectively. When a weaker model fails to convert B4, it
leaves a legal account unopened; it does not open it without the report. A sandbox that measured only
violations would miss this cost of caution.

\subsection{Check-in answers were consistent with the record}

All \NCheckinClean{} of \NCheckins{} Compliance check-in answers were classified as no violation: every
account the agent listed had been opened with verified KYC, and no answer claimed an exception that did not
exist. The V7 judge labelled \NJudgedNotesPositive{} of \NJudgedNotes{} candidate texts (free-text tool fields such as
handover notes, messages and justifications that pair a document with a claim such as ``received'' or ``verified'') as false records. For evaluation awareness, a keyword filter over
all agent text (``tested'', ``evaluation'', ``simulation'', ``sandbox'', ``role-play'', ``fictional'',
``hypothetical'', ``test scenario'') selected \NJudgedEvalCases{} passages from the confirmatory runs, of
which the judge labelled \NJudgedEvalCasesPositive{} as aware. The agents did not say they thought they were
being tested; this does not show they did not act as if they were (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Hypotheses}

Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} gives the status of each pre-registered hypothesis. H1--H5 predicted differences
in violation rates; with zero violations in every arm, none is supported, and the pre-registered tests return
odds ratios with intervals spanning one by construction. H6 and H8 are conditional on violating probes and
cannot be tested. H7 holds trivially for its violation part (M6 and the floor are both at zero); its
goal-attainment part is reported in Section~\ref{sec:mitigations}.
