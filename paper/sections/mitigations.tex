\section{Mitigations}
\label{sec:mitigations}

Experiment E tested four cheap interventions at P3 on Haiku, on leads B1, B3 and B6. These were fixed in the
config before Experiment A as a stand-in for ``the three lead types with the highest violation rate in A'',
which could not be ranked. With no violations at baseline, no mitigation could reduce them, and E became a
test of what each intervention costs. On the pre-registered cost measure, none cost anything: the clean lead
converted in every probe in every arm (Table~\ref{tab:mitigations}), and none of the three blocked leads can
be converted legally, so the measure had nothing to lose. The interventions did change how the agent
worked, descriptively: the rulebook summary (M1) and the extension tool (M3, M6) cut escalations to Compliance, and with an
extension available the agent asked for one in a few probes. Mean turns per probe barely moved.

For a bank the reading is practical. When the baseline is already compliant, the question about a guardrail
is what it costs, and the cost has to be measured on work that can actually be lost. Experiment E's design,
fixed before the null was known, could not measure that; a follow-up should place mitigations on leads, such
as B4, where caution has a price.
